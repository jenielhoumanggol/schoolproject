Study Techniques



Memory Dependent Subjects

For subjects that require a lot of memorization, such as history or biology, consider using the following study techniques to reinforce your memory.

Technique Description
Visual Diagrams Create mind maps, flowcharts, or diagrams to visually organize information and show relationships between concepts.
Hall et al. (1997) showed that learners who generated their own illustrations from text performed equally as well as learners provided with text and illustrations. Both groups outperformed learners only provided with text.
Flashcards Create flashcards for key terms and concepts to facilitate active recall.
Mnemonics Use mnemonic devices to create associations that make information easier to remember.
Feynman Technique Richard Feynman, a world renowned and widely successful theoretical physicist and has won the Nobel Prize in 1965 developed a learning method called Feynman Technique.
It is a method for learning or reviewing a concept by explaining it in simple terms as if teaching it to someone else. The steps are:
  1. Study: Choose a concept you want to learn about.
  2. Teach: Explain the concept in your own words as if teaching it to a child.
  3. Fill the Gaps: Identify any gaps in your understanding and go back to the source material to review.
  4. Simplify: Simplify and use analogies to make the explanation clearer.
Blurting After studying a topic, close your book and write down everything you remember about the topic. This technique helps reinforce memory and identify areas that need more review.
Practice Testing Search or create your own practice quizzes and test yourself regularly on the material you have studied to enhance retention and identify knowledge gaps.
Spaced Repetition Review material at increasing intervals over time to enhance long-term retention.


Problem Solving Subjects

For subjects that focus on problem-solving skills, such as mathematics or physics, the following techniques can help enhance your understanding and application of concepts.

Technique Description
Note Taking in Math: 3 Column Notes Divide your notes into three columns: Concepts/Theorems, Examples/Problems, and Explanations/Steps. This structure helps organize information and clarify problem-solving processes.
Practice Problems Work through a variety of practice problems to apply concepts and improve problem-solving skills.
Visualization For example, learning with two-dimensional number lines can assist students' understanding of fractions (Soni & Okamoto, 2020), and creating drawings for a mathematical modeling problems can help students solve the problems (Rellensmann et al., 2022).


Interpretation Based Subjects

For subjects that require interpretation and critical thinking, such as literature or social studies, consider these study techniques to deepen your understanding.

Technique Description
SQ3R Reading Method The SQ3R method stands for Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. It is a reading comprehension technique that helps you understand and retain information from texts.
Improve Reading Comprehension Practice active reading strategies such as annotating texts, summarizing paragraphs, and asking questions to enhance comprehension and critical thinking skills.
Discussion Groups Join or form discussion groups to engage in conversations about the material. This allows for diverse perspectives and deeper analysis.
